# CS708 — Seminar & Paper Review — PLAN

**Semester:** 7 · **Credits:** 1 · **Level:** Advanced-Professional
**Theory:** 15 hrs · **Seminar:** 15 hrs · **Tools:** arXiv, ACM DL, IEEE Xplore, Zotero, Google Scholar

## Course Format
Students present papers from the recent literature and critique each other's presentations. The goal is to build the habit of reading research critically and communicating it clearly.

## Syllabus

### Unit 1 — Reading a Paper (3 lectures)
1. The three-pass method — skim, read, study
2. Identifying the contribution — what is actually new
3. Reading the method — is the experiment sound?
4. Reading the results — what do the numbers actually say?
5. Reading the related work — how does it position itself?

### Unit 2 — Critical Appraisal (3 lectures)
1. Strengths and weaknesses — a template
2. Threats to validity — internal, external, construct
3. Reproducibility — could you rebuild it?
4. Context — how does it fit with prior work?
5. Significance — why does this matter outside academia?

### Unit 3 — Presenting a Paper (3 lectures)
1. Structure of a paper presentation — 15-minute form
2. Slides for a paper talk — fewer, denser only where needed
3. Communicating contributions honestly — no overclaiming
4. Handling Q&A from a critical audience
5. Presenting papers outside your subfield

### Unit 4 — Reviewing and Being Reviewed (3 lectures)
1. Writing a peer review — kind, specific, actionable
2. What authors do with reviews
3. Running a paper discussion group
4. Building a personal reading pipeline

### Unit 5 — Staying Current (3 lectures)
1. Following research areas — arXiv alerts, Twitter, newsletters
2. Conferences and journals — which ones matter
3. Reading groups and journal clubs
4. Writing about papers — blog posts, notes, tweets

## Practical Labs (15)

1. Pick a paper from the last 2 years in your capstone area
2. First-pass skim — 10-minute summary in `notes/paper-01-skim.md`
3. Deep read — fill a structured review template
4. Write a 500-word critical summary
5. Present the paper — 15 minutes, recorded
6. Critique a peer's presentation — written feedback
7. Read a paper outside your comfort zone (theory, systems, or HCI)
8. Skim three more papers — comparative one-pager
9. Run a paper discussion group — lead a 30-minute session
10. Write a peer review of a submission (use a preprint or the class's own drafts)
11. Read a second paper in depth and present it
12. Peer review a classmate's paper presentation write-up
13. Build a personal reading pipeline — RSS / arXiv alerts / notes
14. Write a blog post summarising one of your read papers
15. Final — a portfolio of 5+ papers read, 2 presented, 2 reviews written
